# SENTINEL NOTE — KESHIK INSPECTION
## Directional Vocabulary Introduction Review

Status: Observational  
Authority: Founder  
Scope: Language survivability only  
Action: None required  

---

### Purpose

This note records a Keshik inspection of newly introduced directional vocabulary
(coreward, rimward, spinward, trailing, Sol-as-reference) used for orientation
within Sentinel systems.

The Keshik evaluates survivability, not correctness.

---

### Inspection Criteria

Each term was evaluated for:

- Coercion risk
- Inflation risk
- Dependency creation
- Load-bearing survivability
- Failure safety

No enforcement or governance implications were considered.

---

### Findings

**Coreward**  
Survives as a relative density indicator.  
Fails safely if misused as authority or value judgment.

**Rimward**  
Survives cleanly.  
Preserves optionality and exploration without dilution.

**Spinward**  
Survives conditionally.  
Must remain descriptive of motion-with-system, not progress or correctness.

**Trailing**  
Survives fully.  
Explains lag without declaring failure or obsolescence.

**Sol (Local Reference Frame)**  
Survives only when explicitly positional.  
Breaks if universalized or treated as a center of authority.

**Constellation / Bridges**  
Survive as recognitional language only.  
Must not be canonized into fixed structures.

**Adamantium Box**  
No drift detected.  
Remains stable as an S³ measurement instrument.

---

### Keshik Verdict

No hostile or coercive terms detected.  
All new vocabulary fails safely if misused.  
Two terms require ongoing scope discipline (Spinward, Sol).

The language introduces orientation without gravity.

---

### Closing

The words do not command.  
They only point.

Those that survive will be reused.  
Those that do not will dim.

Inspection complete.
